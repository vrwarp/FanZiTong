# 繁字通 FanZiTong — learning-effectiveness critique (iter1)

Reviewer lens: second-language acquisition, Chinese literacy pedagogy, heritage-language education, spaced retrieval. The learner's bottleneck is orthographic mapping — binding character shapes to an aural lexicon that already exists — so every exercise is judged on one question: does it force the shape → known sound/meaning link to be retrieved, and is feedback delivered at the grain where the error actually lives (the character)?

No blocker: the pinyin rule is honored on every prompt screen (02, 04, 08, 10, 12, 20), the default is "Manual tap only", and the Vocab list hides pinyin by default. The findings below are about retrieval demand, feedback content and transfer.

## Findings (ordered by severity)

- [major] [08-drill-menu-slip / 09-drill-menu-result] The order slip is a string-matching task, not a reading task — The cue "滷肉飯 (小)、蛋餅、蚵仔煎" is given in characters, so the learner ticks boxes by visually matching identical strings on the slip; the drill can be completed perfectly with zero knowledge of Chinese. No sound or meaning is retrieved anywhere, so the mode contributes nothing to orthographic mapping even though its realia is the most transfer-relevant surface in the app (transfer-appropriate processing: practice must mirror the real task, and in real life the order arrives through the ear — "lǔ ròu fàn, xiǎo de" — and the eyes must find it on paper). Two side effects: the trap rows (魯肉飯, 蚵仔炸) turn red after grading with no explanation of why they are traps, and the 小菜 section lists 蛋餅 twice (the foil generator emitted the target string itself), so ticking the second identical row would be graded as an error — a trust-destroying bug in a timed task. Fix: make the cue sound + meaning, which is exactly the sanctioned exception the foil drill already uses ("Your friend says: lǔ ròu fàn (small), dàn bǐng, … — tick it"), and only show the target strings in the grading step; de-duplicate foil rows against the target; after grading make every row on the slip tap-to-reveal (pinyin + gloss) with an "add to deck" action so unknown items like 焢肉飯, 嘴邊肉, 皮蛋豆腐 become incidental-learning input instead of dead weight. Add an optional "as heard" reading field so items the learner knows by their Taiwanese names (蚵仔煎 ô-á-tsian, 肉圓 bah-uân) can be cued by the sound that is actually in their head. The 20-second timer then measures reading fluency, which is what it should measure.

- [major] [04-study-drill-cloze / 12-drill-cloze / 13-drill-cloze-correct] The cloze never requires reading the sentence — All four options are look-alike misspellings of the same word (鹵肉飯 / 魯肉飯 / 滷內飯 / 滷肉飯; 枚師 / 收師 / 牧師 / 牧帥), so the sentence is decoration: the task collapses into the foil drill in a different frame, and the Drills-tab promise ("use your ear for grammar") cannot be kept because no grammar or meaning distinguishes the options. Worse, the sentence is the identical example shown with full pinyin and translation minutes earlier at the reveal (03 → 04), so the learner is recognising an episode rather than a word, and the visible 滷蛋 in the same sentence lets 滷 be matched by eye. (Same-screen nits: the blank shows two slots for the three-character 滷肉飯 but two for the two-character 牧師; "Pick the character that fits" for multi-character words.) Interleaving and discrimination research says distractors should be what the learner actually confuses at the level being tested; in connected text that is other same-domain words, not misspellings. Fix: draw cloze distractors from other cards in the same domain (牧師 vs 執事 / 弟兄 / 主日; 滷肉飯 vs 牛肉麵 / 蚵仔煎 / 雞排), optionally keeping one visual foil; when the card's own sentence was shown in the last N minutes, source the cloze from another card's sentence that contains the word (滷肉飯's sentence already contains 滷蛋, so the deck cross-references itself) or allow several sentences per card; match blank length to word length; after answering, show pinyin + gloss for each distractor too, so every option is a mini retrieval of a deck word (encoding variability, interleaved retrieval).

- [major] [03-study-revealed / 20-study-revealed-dark] The reveal romanises the whole example sentence and does not mark the target word — After the tap the panel shows the sentence in characters, then the full sentence in pinyin, then English. For a fully pinyin-literate heritage speaker the eye drops straight to the pinyin line and the character line is skipped, which reproduces the exact failure mode the pedagogy rule exists to prevent, one screen later. The example sentence is also the only connected-text exposure per card — the only place to practise locating a word inside an unspaced character string, a real reading sub-skill — yet the target is not highlighted here although the cloze-correct screen (13) already has that styling. Fix: render the sentence in characters with the target word highlighted; put the sentence pinyin behind a "show reading" tap, or per-word tap-to-reveal so the learner asks only for the words they cannot read; keep the English translation visible as a comprehension check. This turns the example into a second, free retrieval attempt instead of a crutch (retrieval practice on connected text; the rule's own rationale).

- [major] [11-drill-foil-wrong] Wrong-pick feedback is verification-only — "不對 — the correct form is 滷肉飯" says which shape, not why: nothing tells the learner that the chosen 內 is nèi "inside" and the target 肉 is ròu "meat", even though naming the foil's sound is the most valuable thing the app could do here — the learner already owns both syllables aurally, so "you picked nèi" instantly binds the wrong shape to its own known sound (orthographic mapping happening on the foil, for free). The drill then moves on with no corrective retrieval. Elaborative feedback beats right/wrong feedback for retention and transfer (Butler, Godbole & Marsh 2013), and an error followed by immediate re-retrieval is corrected far better than one followed by passive viewing. Fix: on a wrong pick show the chosen and correct words side by side, enlarged, with the differing character outlined and glossed (內 nèi vs 肉 ròu; 帥 shuài vs 師 shī; 收 shōu / 枚 méi vs 牧 mù); require tapping the correct tile to continue; re-queue the same item later in the drill; keep the pre-answer hint generic and move specific component notes to post-answer (the current "which radical carries the meaning?" line is a fine prompt but is actually misleading for 魯, see the variants finding).

- [major] [02-study-prompt-hidden / 03-study-revealed] Self-rating is easy to inflate, and FSRS is only as honest as the rating — Three things weaken calibration on the card that drives the schedule. (1) The domain chip (Food / 飲食, Slang / 俚語) is displayed before the reveal: it is a retrieval cue that will not exist on a bulletin or in a LINE thread and, combined with recognising one character (飯), makes "Good" possible without ever mapping 滷 (cue-dependent retrieval; encoding specificity). (2) Nothing requires a commitment before the tap, so the reveal invites hindsight ("I knew that") — the classic failure of self-graded flashcards. (3) The buttons carry only intervals; there is no rubric tying Again / Hard / Good / Easy to the bottleneck, so "recognised half the word from context" and "read it instantly" both get rated Good. Fix: hide the domain chip until reveal (show it in the answer panel); enforce a short minimum dwell before the tap registers and measure reveal latency, showing it beside the buttons ("4.2 s") with a soft nudge toward Hard above a threshold — latency is the one objective fluency signal available offline, and "Rapid Recognition" should actually be rapid; add one-line anchors under the buttons (Again: the word didn't come; Hard: came slowly or only from part of it; Good: sound and meaning came; Easy: instant, would read it in a chat).

- [major] [08-drill-menu-slip / 10-drill-foil / 04-study-drill-cloze / 17-stats] Attested real-world spellings are trained as errors — 魯肉飯 is how a large share of Taiwanese shops actually write lǔ ròu fàn, 鹵 is the standard form in mainland and much converted text, and 借口 is common in casual typing (the card's own gloss "Excuse (Taiwan standard form)" hints at this without ever showing 借口). Using these as "wrong" foils teaches the learner to reject exactly the strings they need to recognise; on the order slip 魯肉飯 turns red as a trap although ticking it at 阿婆小吃店 would get the right dish. This is negative transfer manufactured by the app (ecological validity). Fix: add an "accepted variants / also written" field per card (魯肉飯 | 鹵肉飯; 借口); exclude variants from foil generation; show "also written 魯肉飯 (common on menus)" in the reveal and in feedback; on the menu slip, treat a variant row as an accepted answer with a note, or use it deliberately as a "same dish, different spelling" recognition test rather than an error.

- [major] [03-study-revealed / 15-card-editor] There is no character-level layer — Cards, prompts, foils and stats are all word-level. Word units are right for transfer, but they let a learner get 滷肉飯 "right" from the silhouette plus 飯 while still unable to read 滷 in 滷蛋 or 滷味, and 師 in 師母 after mastering 牧師. Orthographic mapping happens at the character–syllable grain, and this learner already owns every syllable, so per-character binding is precisely the missing link; the deck already contains the co-occurrences to make it visible (滷 in 滷肉飯 / 滷蛋; 肉 in 肉燥飯 / 牛肉麵 / 嘴邊肉; 師 in 牧師 / 師母). Fix: on the reveal make each character of the word tappable to show its own reading, core meaning and "also in: …" computed from the deck; in foil feedback name the component that differs; in Stats add a per-character view (characters appearing in ≥ 2 words, and which of those words lapsed) so a leech can be traced to a character rather than a word. Only a small offline component table is needed, no radical database.

- [minor] [07-drills-tab] Drill outcomes feed FSRS as if they were recall — "Results still feed your FSRS schedule" is well-intentioned, but a correct 4-tile pick has a 25% guess floor and (as designed today) a correct order slip is a string match; treating either as a "Good" review inflates stability and pushes genuine recall reviews further out (recognition ≠ recall; the schedule should be updated by the harder evidence). Fix: apply an asymmetric mapping — a drill failure counts as Again (strong evidence), a drill success only nudges stability for cards in Learning/Relearning and is a no-op for Review cards — and print the applied rating in the drill feedback so the learner understands what just happened to the schedule.

- [minor] [07-drills-tab] Realia exists for one of four domains — The menu slip proves the pattern (find the target inside an authentic layout under time pressure), but church, slang and anime words only ever appear as isolated flashcards plus one sentence, and bulletins and chats are exactly the learner's stated targets. Fix, fully offline and template-based: a 週報 order-of-service list (宣召、敬拜、讀經、證道、奉獻、報告、禱告事項) where the task is "tap the item that is X"; a LINE-style thread built from deck sentences where the task is "which message says X"; a PTT/巴哈 post template for ACGN lingo. Grading and foil placement reuse the menu-slip engine and content comes from existing card sentences, so no new data pipeline is needed (transfer: practise the surface you will read).

- [minor] [15-card-editor / 16-import-preview] Foils and contexts are hand-authored, one sentence per card — Drill quality is only as good as the "Visual foils 形近字" and "Example sentence" fields, and a learner who adds a word from a real menu or bulletin cannot author look-alike foils (they cannot yet see what 滷 is confusable with) and will usually add no sentence, so custom cards silently drop out of the foil and cloze drills — the words that matter most get the least practice. Fix: ship a small offline confusable table (a few hundred pairs: 內/肉, 師/帥, 己/已/巳, 未/末, 牧/收/枚, 燥/躁/操, 旦/蛋, 面/麵) and generate foils by substituting one character; add deck-internal foils (other words sharing a character); allow several example sentences separated with |; add a free-text "notes / mnemonic" field so the learner can write their own hook — a self-generated mnemonic for 藉 will outlast any supplied one (generation effect, encoding variability).

- [minor] [17-stats / 20-study-revealed-dark] Leech handling is "more of the same" — The leech panel shows 藉口 · 4 lapses and a button that launches ordinary drills, and the review card for a leech (20) is identical to any other card. A leech is a diagnosis that repetition is not working: the learner confuses it with a specific foil, cannot parse a specific character (藉 is genuinely dense: 艹 + 耒 + 昔), or is being penalised for a variant (借口). Fix: on the leech row show the lapse history and which foil was chosen when it was missed; open a remediation view with the character breakdown, the confusable, and the notes/mnemonic field pre-focused; offer a "recognise-only / variant-tolerant" state; and make sure "Again" presses during same-session learning steps do not count as lapses, otherwise a normal first-day struggle with a hard character becomes a leech overnight (interference needs differentiated treatment, not more exposures).

- [minor] [05-session-summary] The summary celebrates but does not consolidate — "Cards reviewed 5 · Answers 5 · Retention 80% · Time 2s" ends the session with metrics that are noise at n = 5 (and "Time 2s" is clearly not a real duration) and throws away the cheapest consolidation moment of the day. Fix: list the items rated Again/Hard as characters only (no pinyin, tap-to-reveal) so the last act of the session is one more retrieval on the weak items (spaced re-retrieval at session end); label the number "answers not 'Again'" and suppress the percentage below ~10 answers; show minutes rather than seconds.

Out of scope for this lens but noted: the two "dark" screenshots (19, 20) render in the light palette, so the dark theme could not be evaluated.

Overall score: 6/10 — the recognition loop (character-only prompt, strict tap-to-reveal, FSRS with visible intervals, a well-built foil drill) is the right core for this learner, but two of the four modes create no retrieval demand on the bottleneck and the feedback moments reveal the wrong things at the wrong grain (whole-sentence pinyin, no per-character or contrastive explanation), so today the app trains shape discrimination well and shape → word binding only partially.

## Keep doing
- Character-only prompt with pinyin strictly tap-to-reveal; "Manual tap only (recommended)" as the default with honest explanatory copy in Settings; "Show pinyin" off by default in the Vocab list.
- Reading direction only — no handwriting or production drills; that matches both the learner's bottleneck and the 15–30 minute budget.
- The foil drill's cue design (sound + meaning → shape), foils that vary which character differs (滷內飯 vs 鹵肉飯; 牧帥 vs 枚師), and hand-curated look-alikes of genuinely confusable pairs.
- Drills interleaved every 5th card and "Again" cards returning within the session — short-lag retrieval of just-encoded items is the right first step of expanding practice.
- Menu realia authenticity: red-and-white slip, 小/大 columns, 桌號, sectioned categories, time pressure, and showing the trap rows after grading.
- Interval preview on every rating button and honest metric definitions on Stats ("80% of answers were not 'Again'").
- Target-word highlighting on the cloze-correct screen — extend it to the reveal.
- Domain-tagged, Taiwan-specific starter vocabulary (滷肉飯, 蚵仔煎, 師母, 本命, 藉口) with example sentences that sound like real speech ("老闆，我要一碗大碗的…").
- Vocab management that respects the learner's ownership: import with duplicate detection, per-card FSRS state with reset, full local backup.
